# How Jobs built keynotes: evidence for a slide tool

Jobs never published slide rules. I found no source where he states one. Every principle below is either seen in a transcript (T1) or reported by observers (T2, T3).

**Biggest limit.** I opened no slide archive. T1 here means "seen in a transcript". No words-per-slide figure, font size or background colour is T1. Font size and background colour of Jobs-era slides are UNVERIFIED.

Tiers: T1 = in a transcript I read. T2 = stated by a named expert page I opened. T3 = blog or wiki with no named source. Keys S1 to S29 resolve to URLs in section 4.

## 2. Structure of a Jobs talk

- **Opening.** Macworld 2007 spends 26 minutes on Mac, iPod and Apple TV before the iPhone (S1). The iMac 1998 talk opens with company numbers (S3). Stanford opens: "I want to tell you three stories from my life. That's it. No big deal. Just three stories." (S4).
- **Rule of three.** Jobs lists three products, then says "These are not three separate devices. This is one device." (S1). Gallo says a Jobs talk "is typically divided into three parts" (S8) and cites three iPad 2 improvements (S9).
- **Problem and solution.** The smartphone chart and the fixed-button argument (S1). Duarte says she found a push from what is toward what could be (S18). Her iPhone analysis itself was not available to me (S19 is a summary).
- **Demo.** About a third of the keynote (row 9). Live calls to Jony Ive and a Starbucks (S1).
- **"One more thing."** Weak evidence (row 15). The iPhone reveal came early, not last (S1).
- **Close.** An anchor quote (row 14).
- **Preparation.** Rehearsal at full intensity, with feedback mid-run (S12).

## 3. Transfer to a scientific talk

**Transfers.** The rule of three. The problem-first frame. A simple labelled chart. One idea per slide. Numbers restated against a baseline. Rehearsal. Plain speech.

**Does not transfer.**
- *Product reveal.* A data talk has no secrecy or launch surprise.
- *Persuasion over information.* Tufte says slideware sells instead of informs (S20). A former employee called the keynotes part sales pitch (S22).
- *Sparse slides for data.* Tufte: "Why are our presentations operating at 2% of the data richness of routine tables found in the sports section?" (S21). He prefers written reports and handouts.
- *Unsourced numbers.* Jobs gave figures such as "over a quarter billion users" with no source (S1). Reviewers of data need n, uncertainty and a source.
- *Hyperbole.* "Revolutionary" and "works like magic" (S1) fail peer scrutiny.
- *Pace.* Jobs showed 9 screens in 70 seconds (S15). Bourne advises at most one visual per minute (S28).
- *Slides for thinking.* A reader comment on Tufte's site quotes Isaacson (p. 337): Jobs banned slide decks in product reviews (S21). T3, second-hand, not checked against the book.

Tufte's Wired essay does not name Jobs (I read it, S20). I found no Tufte text on Jobs's slides. Any Tufte-versus-Jobs framing is UNVERIFIED. Reynolds himself calls presentation "as much art as science" (S15).

**Five testable rules.** The thresholds are my proposals, except the number in rule 4, which comes from S28.

1. **Claim headline and word cap.** Each body slide has one sentence headline of at most 15 words that states a claim. Total slide text is at most 25 words, excluding figure labels. Basis: assertion-evidence design (S27), Gallo's 18-word maximum (S11). Check: script word count.
2. **No bullets.** Zero bullet markers. One list of at most three parallel items is allowed. Basis: S10, S11, S28 (three key points). Check: script.
3. **One figure per slide, with units.** Each figure has labelled axes with units, plus n and an uncertainty measure in the caption. Basis: Tufte's dequantification complaint (S21). Check: reviewer checklist; script reads captions.
4. **Pace.** Distinct slides, counting builds, divided by planned minutes is at most 1.0. Basis: S28. Check: script.
5. **Three take-homes with baselines.** At most three take-home claims, stated by slide 3 and repeated on the last slide. Each number has a baseline: control, prior value or chance. Basis: Jobs's comparisons (S1), S28. Check: script counts claims; reviewer checks baselines.
