## 2. Assertion-evidence (AE) structure

- Headline: one complete sentence that states the message, at most two lines (S6). It replaces the phrase headline (S2).
- Evidence area: a photo, drawing, graph, table, diagram, film, or words arranged visually (S2). Use one or two call-outs at most (S6). Secondary detail goes to the speaker notes (S7).
- Removed: the topic phrase, the bullet list, and the default text placeholder (S2, S6).
- Talk frame: a mapping slide with one sentence and one picture per section; three to five sections (S6).
- Results: P1 to P3 hold the main data. 2011 (55 vs 56 students): immediate essay p=0.098, delayed comprehension p=0.038 (S3). 2012 (48 vs 52): essay difference not statistically significant (S4). A presenter study (AE builders understood more) lists no sample size (S8).

## 3. What the evidence does not show

1. Bundled treatment. Every AE test changes headline, body, word count and builds together. No study isolates one element.
2. Audience. Participants were undergraduates, or mostly postsecondary in the meta-analyses. I found no AE test with working scientists. A 2024 pilot reports that Mayer's principles can work for experts. Its methods and N are paywalled (S28).
3. Replication. The 2012 repeat missed significance. Its bullet group scored higher than in 2011, perhaps from more animation (S4).
4. Imbalance. In S2 the AE group rated its own prior knowledge higher (2.87 vs 2.24 of 7) despite random assignment.
5. Outcome. Tests cover one 6 to 10 minute recorded talk on one causal process, retention at about a week, essays scored by two authors (S2). Statistical or evaluative talks are untested (S3).
6. Authorship. Alley co-wrote the AE studies. Farkas calls Alley partisan, the control slides poor and the findings modest. He accepts sentence titles. He rejects a visual on every slide and the claim that bullets harm (S10). A blog critique repeats the confound point (S11).
7. Facts and nulls. Bullets helped recall of secondary statistics (P3). A secondary review reports no learning gain from slides versus no slides in most studies, and equal test scores for AE and bullets in one self-selected veterinary class (S29).
8. Mayer. Effect sizes come from the originator's lab, short narrated lessons and novices. A search snippet gives later-edition values (coherence 0.86, signaling 0.41): UNVERIFIED.
9. Redundancy conflict. Spoken plus written text beat spoken-only (S18). A 63-study review calls the results mixed (S20). Sweller says effects vanish when intrinsic load is low (S22).
10. Preference. Learners prefer more text (S17, S2). Decks read without a speaker are untested.
11. Not found: font-minimum, chart-type or palette effects on learning; Kording talk guidance; Doumont's 2005 article; Alley checklist figures (28 pt, 20 words per minute; snippet only).

## 4. Ten checks for a .pptx script or an outline reviewer

1. Title is a sentence: 6 to 20 words, contains a verb, not "Results" or "Methods" (P1).
2. Each content slide holds a picture, native chart, table or diagram (P2).
3. Flag any list of three or more bulleted paragraphs (P2).
4. Words per slide, excluding title: warn above 25, fail above 40 (P12, P13). Both limits are my defaults.
5. Flag more than 6 objects on a slide. Flag more than one chart or panel (P12, P15).
6. Flag body runs below 20 pt (source lines excepted). Flag text-to-fill contrast below 4.5:1, my choice (P14).
7. Flag a native chart with a legend and four or fewer series, a chart with no highlight shape, and more than two text boxes beside a picture (P8, P10, P12).
8. Flag an image on three or more slides with area under 5% of the slide, and any background picture (P7).
9. Outline: titles read in order as the argument. Slide 2 states the main message and at most five sections. Slides do not exceed talk minutes. Need precedes findings (P4, P5, P6).
10. Flag a slide where more than 80% of its words appear in the speaker notes (P9). Flag entrance animation on text boxes (P11).
